# Character progression and short-video research

Accessed 10 October 2026 at 15:04 UTC. Finch and Habitica product claims below come from their official pages. TikTok recommendations come from TikTok for Business. Any Do It positioning is a hypothesis; none of the sources establish how these mechanics perform on TikTok.

## What existing apps do

[Finch](https://finchcare.com/about-finch) ties a virtual bird's growth to personal care: complete goals to earn energy, send the bird on an adventure, and receive a story or discovery. Users can [dress the bird](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37935977276813-Shops-in-Finch-Outfits-Travel-and-More), decorate its home, and earn seasonal rewards. Friend features focus on encouragement and shared goals. Finch's emotional promise is a gentle companion that makes small self-care steps feel rewarding.

[Habitica](https://habitica.com/) makes real-life tasks part of a game. Its official page says task completion levels up an avatar and unlocks equipment, pets, skills, and quests; friends can form parties and battle monsters. The emotional promise is playful achievement and a visible game identity built through task completion.

Character development and habit-linked rewards are therefore familiar mechanics. A pet that grows with self-care or an RPG avatar that levels up cannot carry Do It's differentiation alone.

## Do It's grounded angle

The approved product direction in issues #33-38 is narrower: two player avatars, cosmetic upgrades unlocked through lifetime Karma and some achievements, body color tied to the app accent, and no score advantage from cosmetics. The original art direction belongs to the girlfriend; later two-character animation may depict the actual Stake result. There is no pet-care loop or combat system in the approved plan. This progression is planned, not a shipped feature.

The marketing hypothesis is that a pair of distinct characters can make a two-person habit story easy to follow: each look belongs to a real player with separate goals; both plans are matched to equal available weekly points; the pair chooses a real-world stake; then the weekly leaderboard shows the outcome and the pair records the Stake result. Lifetime cosmetic progression can persist while weekly competition resets. Keep claims precise: equal available points do not mean equal effort, and cosmetic unlocks do not improve a player's score.

That combination separates the story from Finch's solo companion journey and Habitica's broad RPG world. Test whether two recognizable avatars make Do It's pair rivalry easier to understand and share. Whether players value upgrades, post clips, invite a partner, or return for another week is unknown.

## Three short-video hooks

TikTok's official [Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes) recommends a hook, body, and close, and says the hook should create interest through suspense, surprise, or emotion. Its [creative guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok_creative_accelerator/pc/en) recommends demonstrating the product in use. Treat those as formats to test, not a promise of reach.

1. **"We picked totally different habits. How is this fair?"** Open on the two characters beside "running" and "reading"; show each weekly point target matching, then the stake and leaderboard.
2. **"My character got this because I kept showing up."** Reveal a cosmetic unlocked with lifetime Karma; cut to the weekly scorecard so viewers see that the look is earned and has no scoring advantage.
3. **"I'm working on Swedish. She's working on basketball. Winner picks dinner."** Show both avatars, their separate plans, matched weekly potential, and the agreed stake resolving at week's end.

Use only actual app footage and an actual pair's goals and agreed stake. Until cosmetic progression is implemented, label the character sequence as a concept or prototype instead of implying availability.

## Small experiment

Run three short creative variants with the same audience, spend, landing page, and invitation flow. Use the hooks above, with one focused on the character unlock and two showing the pair's plans and fair scoring. Measure **new paired activations per unique landing-page visitor**: the invited partner accepts, both finish onboarding and confirm habit plans, and both record a first check-in within seven days. Track view-through, clicks, invite sends, and invite acceptance only to diagnose drop-off. Compare repeat check-ins in week two as a secondary signal. Start with a small, directional test and interview a few activated and dropped-off pairs. Do not declare success from views or clicks, and do not infer virality from a small sample.
