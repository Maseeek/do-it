# Competitor messaging and positioning notes

Accessed 9 October 2026 (UTC). First-party product pages and help pages were reviewed on this date. Headlines below are short excerpts; audience descriptions are my inference from the pages unless stated otherwise.

## What the market promises

Habit apps sell a few emotional outcomes: gentler self-care, a more capable version of yourself, fun through game mechanics, social encouragement, or pressure with a consequence. Social accountability and head-to-head habit play already exist. The plausible opening for Do It is narrower: a two-person contest in which each person picks different habits, while the scoring system targets the same available weekly points and a mutually chosen stake gives the round a payoff. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a claim of uniqueness. Equal available points do not mean equal effort, and the balance can change when habits are edited.

## Positioning assessment

The hypothesis survives only in a qualified form. Do It calculates weekly point potential and balances an active habit plan to the other player's target during onboarding. That supports "different habits, equal available weekly points" at setup. It does not support "equally hard" or "always fair," especially after habits change. Weekly, monthly, and yearly leaderboards plus mutual stakes are also in the product context. LifeMasters and Notion Habit Heroes show that duels and period scoring already exist.

Do not market this as the first or only two-person habit competition. Lead with the user problem: "We want to improve different parts of our lives, but still compete fairly." Explain the fairness precisely: different routines can be calibrated to the same available weekly points. Make the stake concrete and user-chosen, such as the winner choosing dinner, while avoiding an implication that a cash wager is required.

Test close friends as the lead audience hypothesis, with couples as a secondary segment. DuoDucks and PairHabit already claim couples/two-person territory; friends can relate to playful rivalry without a promise of relationship repair. Do It's pairing model allows any pair, and distinct habits suit friends with separate goals. Validate before adopting an audience identity.

## Headline concepts

These are original concepts, not competitor quotes.

1. **Different habits. Same shot at the win.** Best product fit: translates plan balancing into understandable competitive fairness without promising equal effort. Supporting copy: "Pick what you want to work on. Do It balances your available weekly points, then you and a friend choose what the winner earns." CTA: **Set up your first duel**.
2. **Make your goals a two-person game.** Broad, friendly, and suitable for close friends or couples. Supporting copy: "Choose your own habits, check in through the week, and see who comes out ahead." CTA: **Invite your person**.
3. **Your habits. Their challenge. Something on the line.** Connects personal choice, a duel, and a stake; avoid any implication of mandatory money. Supporting copy: "Set different routines, agree on a weekly reward, and compete on points." CTA: **Choose a stake**.
4. Two routines. One leaderboard. One winner.
5. Get better at your thing. See how you stack up.
6. The friend who makes you follow through.
7. Pick your habits. Choose your stake. Play the week.
8. A little friendly pressure for the goals you keep postponing.

Ranked first three because they accurately describe separate habit plans, weekly point calibration, invite-led pairing, and chosen stakes. Number 1 is the clearest differentiated claim; number 2 has the broadest audience fit; number 3 makes the reward mechanic vivid. Validate that users understand "same shot" as equal available points, not equal workload.

## Simple message test

Randomize close-friend prospects between headlines 1 and 2, keeping copy and invite flow constant. Count an invite as qualified only when its recipient accepts, completes pairing, confirms a habit plan, and records a first check-in within seven days. Primary measure: qualified partner activations per unique visitor. Track acceptance and check-in separately to locate drop-off. Clicks and invite sends are diagnostic only. Treat a small sample as directional and ask a few pairs who drop off what confused them.
